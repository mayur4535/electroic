About This Calculator

Estimate inductance of rectangular wire loops.

This calculator is useful for rectangular conductor loops, PCB current loops, RF loops and EMI-sensitive paths.

A rectangular loop has inductance because current flowing around the loop creates magnetic flux. Wider and taller loops generally increase inductance, while conductor diameter affects the logarithmic part of the estimate. This is important in switching power layouts, pulse circuits, grounding loops, antenna experiments and loop sensors.

Width WLong horizontal dimension of the loop.
Height HVertical loop dimension.
Diameter dConductor thickness used by the formula.
Turns NInductance scales with turns squared.
Tip: Keep high-current switching loops physically small to reduce unwanted loop inductance and voltage spikes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is rectangular loop inductance?

It is the inductance created by current flowing around a rectangular loop conductor.

What value should I use for relative permeability?

Use μr = 1 for air or non-magnetic surroundings.

Why does wire diameter matter?

Wire diameter changes the magnetic field distribution close to the conductor and appears in the logarithmic part of the formula.

Can I use this for PCB traces?

It can give a rough estimate, but PCB trace geometry and return path can change real inductance.

Does more turns increase inductance?

Yes. The formula multiplies by turns squared.

Is this result exact?

No. It is an engineering estimate and should be verified for precision RF or pulse designs.
