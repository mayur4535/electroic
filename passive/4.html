Diagrams & Theory

A quartz crystal behaves like a mechanical resonator with an electrical equivalent circuit. The motional branch uses R, L and C in series, while the static capacitance appears in parallel across the crystal terminals.

Formulas & Mathematical Logic

Step 1: Convert Cs from fF to F: Cs = Cs × 10^-15.
Step 2: Convert Cp from pF to F: Cp = Cp × 10^-12.
Step 3: Convert Ls from mH to H: Ls = Ls × 10^-3.
Step 4: Series resonant frequency: Fs = 1 / (2 × π × sqrt(Ls × Cs)).
Step 5: Parallel capacitance combination: Cmix = (Cs × Cp) / (Cs + Cp).
Step 6: Parallel resonant frequency: Fp = 1 / (2 × π × sqrt(Ls × Cmix)).
Step 7: Crystal Q factor: Q = 1 / (2 × π × Fs × Rs × Cs).
Step 8: Pulling factor: Pulling Factor = Cp / Cs.

About This Calculator

The Quartz Crystal Parameter Q-Factor Frequency Calculator by CalcBoy helps estimate the main equivalent circuit values used to understand quartz crystal resonators. A quartz crystal is not only a simple two-terminal component; electrically, it can be represented by a motional resistance, motional inductance and motional capacitance in series, with a static parallel capacitance across the terminals. These parameters influence the series resonant frequency, parallel resonant frequency, Q factor and pulling behavior of the crystal.

This CalcBoy quartz crystal calculator accepts series resistance Rs, series capacitance Cs, parallel capacitance Cp and series inductance Ls. The series resonance frequency is calculated from Ls and Cs. The parallel resonance frequency uses the combined effect of series capacitance and parallel capacitance. The Q-factor result indicates how sharp or selective the resonance is. A higher Q crystal has lower losses and a narrower resonance bandwidth, which is important in oscillator stability, RF filtering and frequency reference circuits. The pulling factor gives a simple capacitance ratio used to understand how load capacitance may influence the crystal frequency.

This calculator is useful for oscillator design, crystal filter analysis, RF frequency reference planning, microcontroller clock design, resonator comparison and electronics education. It can help users compare crystals from datasheets or estimate behavior from equivalent circuit values. Practical oscillator circuits also depend on load capacitance, drive level, ESR, package parasitics, temperature drift, aging, PCB layout and amplifier gain margin. For production design, the calculator should be used together with the manufacturer datasheet, oscillator circuit testing and frequency measurement.
