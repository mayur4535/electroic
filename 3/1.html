About This Calculator

Standardize pressure readings across diverse mechanical and industrial systems.

The CalcBoy Units of Pressure Converter translates pressure units instantly, generating standardized equivalents across international, imperial, and metric standards.

Pressure measurements are highly localized across engineering disciplines. While international scientific systems rely on Pascal (Pa) or Kilopascal (kPa) metrics, hydraulic and automotive frameworks often utilize Pound per Square Inch (PSI) and Bar units. Similarly, meteorological studies measure pressure in Inches of Mercury (inHg) or Atmospheres (atm). This calculator bridges these different measurement standards into a single, unified interface.

The conversion system operates by mapping units relative to a central base unit (PSI) using precise physical conversion factors. For example, standard atmospheric pressure at sea level is equivalent to 1 Atmosphere, 14.69595 PSI, 1.01325 Bar, or 101.325 kPa. By standardizing these units, engineers can perform conversions easily without risk of rounding errors.

Using this calculator, mechanical engineers, HVAC installers, fluid dynamicists, and science students can execute calculations quickly and reliably across both metric and imperial systems.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the standard SI unit of pressure?

The standard SI unit of pressure is the Pascal (Pa), defined as one Newton per square meter (N/m²). For practical industrial applications, the Kilopascal (kPa) or Bar is more commonly used.

2. How does Feet of Water relate to PSI?

Feet of Water (ft H₂O) measures hydrostatic pressure. One foot of water column exerts approximately 0.4335 PSI at its base. Therefore, multiplying feet of water by 0.4335 yields the pressure in PSI.

3. What is the difference between gauge pressure and absolute pressure?

Gauge pressure measures pressure relative to the surrounding atmospheric pressure, reading 0 in standard air. Absolute pressure measures pressure relative to a perfect vacuum, incorporating atmospheric pressure (approx. 14.7 PSI at sea level).

4. How is Inches of Mercury (inHg) used?

Inches of Mercury is a non-SI unit historically derived from mercury barometers. It is commonly used in meteorology, aviation, and HVAC vacuum testing, where standard sea-level atmospheric pressure is equal to 29.92 inHg.

5. What do KSC and KSM represent?

KSC stands for Kilogram-force per Square Centimeter (kgf/cm²), a metric unit commonly used in older European and Asian industrial machinery. KSM stands for Kilogram-force per Square Meter (kgf/m²), used in highly specific low-pressure or HVAC applications.

6. Is standard atmospheric pressure exactly 1 Bar?

No, standard atmospheric pressure is slightly higher than 1 Bar. One standard atmosphere (1 atm) is defined as exactly 101,325 Pascals, which is equivalent to 1.01325 Bar or 14.69595 PSI.
