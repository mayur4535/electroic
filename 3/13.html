Input Parameters Specification

Ambient Temperature (Ta)The air temperature immediately surrounding the heatsink assembly, representing the cooling medium.
Junction Temperature (Tj)The internal silicon die temperature of the transistor or processor. Must stay below safety limits to avoid silicon failure.
Package-to-Case Resistance (Rjc)Conductive thermal resistance inside the component. Defined by semiconductor manufacturer packaging design.
Interface Material Resistance (Rcs)The contact thermal resistance between device surface and heatsink surface, minimized using thermal paste.
Heatsink-to-Ambient (Rsa)The convective and radiative thermal dissipation factor of the heatsink fin geometry, indicating dissipation power.

Diagrams & Theory

A semiconductor cooling model is solved like an electrical circuit where temperature represents voltage, power represents current, and thermal resistance represents electrical resistance.

Formulas & Mathematical Logic

Total Thermal Resistance: Rtotal = Rjc + Rcs + Rsa
Max Power Dissipation: P = (Tj - Ta) / Rtotal
Junction Temperature: Tj = P × Rtotal + Ta
Case Temperature: Tc = Tj - (P × Rjc)
Heatsink Temperature: Ts = Ta + (P × Rsa)

Step-by-Step Example

Example: Ambient Temperature = 40 °C, Max Junction Temperature = 125 °C, Rjc = 1.4 °C/W, Rcs = 0.6 °C/W, Rsa = 3.0 °C/W.
Step 1: Calculate total thermal resistance. Rtotal = 1.4 + 0.6 + 3.0 = 5.0 °C/W.
Step 2: Calculate maximum safe power dissipation. P = (125 - 40) / 5.0 = 17 W.
Step 3: Verify case temperature Tc at this power level. Tc = 125 - (17 × 1.4) = 101.2 °C.
Step 4: Verify heatsink temperature Ts at this power level. Ts = 40 + (17 × 3.0) = 91.0 °C.

About This Calculator

Model physical semiconductor heat flow and verify thermal budgets.

The CalcBoy Heat Sink Design Calculator is a tool designed to model heat flow through package components to optimize cooling properties.

In power semiconductor engineering, heat is the primary enemy of silicon longevity. This calculator simulates heat flow out of devices like transistors, MOSFETs, IGBTs, CPUs, power diodes, and solid-state relays. Solid thermal design prevents rapid degradation, performance throttling, and thermal runaway.

Using the electrical resistance analogy where temperature rise mimics Ohm's Law potential drop, the calculator tracks the total thermal path. Entering manufacturer details lets engineers decide whether native case dissipation is enough or if custom coolers, interface pads, and fan cooling modifications are required.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is junction temperature in thermal design?

Junction temperature represents the core silicon temperature inside the semiconductor die. Exceeding its maximum limit typically leads to component destruction or severe degradation.

2. How does thermal resistance relate to electrical resistance?

Thermal resistance mimics Ohm's law, where temperature represents voltage, power represents current, and thermal resistance represents electrical resistance. The drop across layers determines surface temperatures.

3. Why is case-to-sink thermal resistance important?

Case-to-sink resistance represents the quality of the thermal interface compound layer. Air gaps or uneven clamping create micro-voids, trapping heat at the component casing.

4. What does a lower heatsink-to-ambient resistance mean?

A lower heatsink-to-ambient resistance value implies a more efficient heatsink design, indicating it can move heat to ambient air faster per watt of dissipated energy.

5. Can this calculator determine heatsink size?

It determines the maximum allowable thermal resistance of the heatsink. Designers then select heatsink physical profiles, surface areas, and dimensions matching that resistance limit.

6. Why must we keep junction temperature below maximum ratings?

Semiconductor reliability drops exponentially as temperature increases. Working near maximum limits increases hot carrier injection risks and die stress failure rates.
