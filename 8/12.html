Formulas & Mathematical Logic

Primary to Secondary Turns Ratio Formulation (Np / Ns):
Np / Ns = sqrt(Input Impedance / Output Impedance)
Secondary to Primary Inverse Turns Ratio (Ns / Np):
Ns / Np = 1 / sqrt(Input Impedance / Output Impedance)

The transform framework applies the physical square-law induction rule to compute electrical winding relationships necessary to counter impedance step alterations directly.

Step-by-Step Practical Calculation Example

Suppose you need to match a standard RF transmitter output to a long-wire antenna:
• Input Impedance (Zi) = 50 Ω
• Output Impedance (Zl) = 450 Ω

Step 1: Calculate the impedance ratio and solve the Primary-to-Secondary turns ratio (Np/Ns).

Np / Ns = sqrt( Zi / Zl )
Np / Ns = sqrt( 50 / 450 )
Np / Ns = sqrt( 0.1111111 ) ≈ 0.3333333

This means a 1:3 winding turns ratio is required (for every 1 turn on the primary coil, you need 3 turns on the secondary coil).

Step 2: Solve the inverse Secondary-to-Primary turns ratio (Ns/Np).

Ns / Np = 1 / (Np / Ns)
Ns / Np = 1 / 0.3333333 = 3.0000000

This confirms that a 1:9 impedance matching transformer requires a 1:3 turns ratio winding structure.

About This Calculator

Optimize impedance matching networks using high-frequency RF winding ratios.

The CalcBoy RF Transformer Calculator evaluates the primary-to-secondary turns ratio (Np/Ns) and the secondary-to-primary turns ratio (Ns/Np) needed to achieve perfect impedance matching between circuits.

In high-frequency radio frequency (RF) design, transferring power efficiently between components is a fundamental engineering requirement. Active circuits like power amplifiers, mixers, and receivers often exhibit different characteristic impedances than passive structures like antennas or transmission lines (typically 50 or 75 Ohms). Connecting two mismatched interfaces directly causes signal reflections, standing waves (high VSWR), and significant insertion loss. An RF transformer uses magnetic flux coupling to transform voltage and current levels, providing a passive, low-loss method to match these impedances.

Unlike low-frequency power transformers, RF transformers operate across megahertz and gigahertz bands, utilizing specialized ferrite toroid or binocular cores to achieve high magnetic coupling coefficients with minimal losses. The math behind this transformation relies on the square-law induction relationship: the impedance ratio between the primary and secondary windings is proportional to the square of the winding turns ratio. This calculator solves this relationship, helping antenna designers and hardware engineers specify balun designs, matching networks, and circuit board interfaces with precision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the transformer turns ratio related to the square root of the impedance ratio?

Because voltage scales proportionally with the turns ratio (Vp / Vs = Np / Ns) and current scales inversely (Ip / Is = Ns / Np). Since electrical impedance is the ratio of voltage to current (Z = V / I), the impedance ratio scales with the square of the turns ratio (Zp / Zs = (Np / Ns)^2). Taking the square root gives the winding turns ratio.

What is an RF Balun, and how does it relate to these calculations?

A "Balun" (Balanced-to-Unbalanced) is a specialized RF transformer designed to interface balanced signal lines (like folded dipoles) with unbalanced lines (like coaxial cables). Standard 4:1 impedance baluns utilize a 2:1 winding turns ratio to match a 200 Ohm balanced antenna to a 50 Ohm unbalanced coaxial feedline.

Why do RF transformers use ferrite cores instead of iron?

At high frequencies, ordinary iron cores suffer from extreme eddy current and hysteresis power losses. Ferrite materials exhibit very high electrical resistivity along with high magnetic permeability, which minimizes core heating and allows efficient energy transfer across megahertz and gigahertz frequency bands.

How does winding style (like bifilar or trifilar) affect RF transformer performance?

Wrapping the primary and secondary wires tightly together in parallel (bifilar) minimizes leakage inductance and parasitic capacitance. This close physical coupling is crucial for maintaining wide operating bandwidths and minimizing high-frequency insertion loss.

Can I use this calculator for center-tapped RF transformers?

Yes. If the transformer has a center tap, the calculated turns ratio applies to the entire winding. If you are matching to only one side of the tap (half-winding), the matched impedance will be one-fourth of the total secondary impedance due to the square-law relationship.

What limits the maximum power rating of an RF transformer?

Power handling is restricted primarily by the magnetic saturation threshold of the ferrite core. Exceeding this limit causes non-linear distortion, introduces harmonics, and generates excessive core heat, which can damage the wire insulation.
