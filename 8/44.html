Step-by-Step Example

Example: Operating Frequency = 2.4 GHz, Dielectric Constant (Er) = 4.40, Velocity of Propagation (VoP) = 0 (ignored).
Step 1: Check your input parameters. Frequency is 2.4 GHz, which is already in the baseline calculation unit of GHz.
Step 2: Calculate the square root of the dielectric constant (relative permittivity) to find the refractive propagation index: sqrt(Er) = sqrt(4.40) = 2.097618.
Step 3: Solve the guided TEM wave phase velocity relative to free-space: v_phase = 300 / sqrt(Er) = 300 / 2.097618 = 143.0189 mm-GHz (or mm/ns).
Step 4: Solve the physical wavelength (lambda) by dividing the phase velocity by the operating frequency: lambda = v_phase / f = 143.0189 / 2.4 = 59.591 mm.
Result: The calculated guided Transverse Electromagnetic (TEM) wavelength is exactly 59.591 mm, defining the physical dimensions of resonant elements.

About This Calculator

Calculate guided transverse electromagnetic wave dimensions across transmission lines and board traces.

The CalcBoy Wavelength (TEM) Calculator computes the physical wavelength (mm) of a Transverse Electromagnetic (TEM) signal using operating frequency, substrate dielectric constant, or velocity of propagation parameters.

Transverse Electromagnetic (TEM) waves are a class of electromagnetic propagation waves characterized by having both their electric (E) and magnetic (H) field vector components directed completely orthogonal (perpendicular) relative to the direction of propagation. In free space (vacuum) or open air, all electromagnetic waves travel as TEM waves at the speed of light. Inside practical metallic guided-wave transmission lines, such as coaxial cables, microstrip traces, strip lines, and parallel-wire lines, signals also propagate in the dominant TEM mode. Because TEM waves have flat, dispersionless velocity profiles across wide frequency bands, they are highly favored in high-speed digital routing and broadband microwave systems.

However, when an electromagnetic wave propagates through a physical dielectric substrate instead of a vacuum, its phase velocity is slowed down. This velocity reduction is determined by the relative permittivity of the substrate (dielectric constant, Er), which forces the wave to travel at a fraction of the speed of light—a value known as the Velocity of Propagation (VoP). As the phase velocity slows, the physical wavelength of the signal at any given frequency is shortened proportionally. This calculator resolves these parameters, allowing RF antenna designers, high-speed PCB engineers, and transmission line planners to determine the exact wavelength of guided signals, which is a critical step in designing quarter-wave matching transformers, resonant stubs, and RF filters.

Frequently Asked Questions

What physically is a Transverse Electromagnetic (TEM) wave?

A TEM wave is an electromagnetic wave where both the electric and magnetic field components are perpendicular to each other and perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. Unlike TE or TM modes in waveguides, TEM waves do not have any field components directed along the axis of travel, allowing them to propagate down to 0 Hz (DC).

Why does the dielectric constant of a substrate shorten the wavelength?

The dielectric constant (relative permittivity, Er) measures how much a material polarizes in response to an electric field, which slows down the phase velocity of the electromagnetic wave. Because wavelength is the ratio of phase velocity to frequency, a slower phase velocity directly shortens the physical wavelength of the wave.

What is the relationship between the Dielectric Constant and Velocity of Propagation (VoP)?

The two parameters are mathematically equivalent representations of wave slowing. The Velocity of Propagation (expressed as a percentage of the speed of light) is inversely proportional to the square root of the dielectric constant: VoP % = 100 / sqrt(Er). The calculator uses this exact relationship to let you input whichever parameter is known.

Why do high-speed PCB designers care about the guided wavelength?

High-speed digital signals contain high-frequency components with short wavelengths. If a PCB trace is longer than a fraction of the guided wavelength, it behaves as a transmission line rather than a simple wire. This introduces wave reflections, cross-talk, and signal distortion unless the trace impedance is matched.

Can this calculator be used for hollow rectangular or circular waveguides?

No. Hollow metallic waveguides do not support TEM waves because they require at least two separate conductors (like a coaxial cable or microstrip trace) to establish the necessary boundary conditions. Hollow waveguides support Transverse Electric (TE) or Transverse Magnetic (TM) waves, which exhibit high frequency dispersion.

How does operating frequency affect the guided wavelength?

Wavelength is inversely proportional to frequency. As the operating frequency increases, the period of the alternating wave decreases, resulting in a physically shorter wavelength. This is why microwave and millimeter-wave circuits are so compact compared to low-frequency radio systems.
