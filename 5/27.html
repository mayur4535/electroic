Diagrams & Theory

Polyester film capacitors (often referred to as Mylar or "chiclet" capacitors) feature a dielectric layer made from polyethylene terephthalate (PET) film interleaved with metal foil electrodes.

Because their outer epoxy casing is rounded and smooth, color bands are printed horizontally. Read the bands starting from the very top of the capacitor body down towards the radial leads.

Formulas & Mathematical Logic

Capacitance Value: Capacitance (pF) = (Digit 1 × 10 + Digit 2) × Multiplier
Conversion Ratios: 1,000 pF = 1 nF | 1,000,000 pF = 1 µF

Step-by-Step Example

Example: 5-Band Film - Brown (1), Black (0), Orange (x1000), Green (±5%), Red (250V)
Step 1: Identify base digits from Band 1 & 2: Digit 1 = 1, Digit 2 = 0. Base number is 10.
Step 2: Multiply by multiplier in Band 3: 10 × 1,000 = 10,000 pF = 10 nF = 0.01 µF.
Step 3: Read tolerance (Band 4) = Green, which represents a ±5% variation allowance.
Step 4: Read DC rating (Band 5) = Red, which maps to a safe working ceiling of 250 Volts DC.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Why do polyester capacitors use color bands instead of printed numbers?

Printed text on thin polyester film is susceptible to smudging and degradation from heat during soldering. Color bands run entirely around the circumference, remaining readable from any angle.

2. What is the difference between Mylar and Polyester capacitors?

Mylar is a registered brand name for polyethylene terephthalate (PET) film. They refer to the same polyester dielectric structure, offering high insulation resistance.

3. How is the voltage band interpreted on 4-band components?

On 4-band Polyester capacitors, the fourth band indicates the working voltage instead of tolerance. It defaults to baseline low-voltage limits unless specified by the manufacturer.

4. Are polyester film capacitors polarized?

No, polyester film capacitors are non-polarized components. They can be connected in either direction inside AC or DC electrical circuit networks.
