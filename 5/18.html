Formulas & Mathematical Logic

For 3 & 4-Band: Value = (Digit 1 × 10 + Digit 2) × Multiplier (µH)
For 5-Band: Value = (Digit 2 × 10 + Digit 3) × Multiplier (µH) [First Wide Band is Silver]
Minimum tolerance limit: Lmin = Value - (Value × Tolerance / 100)
Maximum tolerance limit: Lmax = Value + (Value × Tolerance / 100)

Step-by-Step Example

Example: 4-Band Inductor - Yellow (4), Violet (7), Black (x1), Gold (±5%)
Step 1: Write down the primary digits: Digit 1 = 4, Digit 2 = 7. Combine them to get: 47.
Step 2: Multiply the combined digits by the multiplier band value: 47 × 1 = 47 µH.
Step 3: Factor in the tolerance to find the operational range: 47 µH ± 5% gives an acceptable window of 44.65 µH to 49.35 µH.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I read an axial mold inductor color code?

Locate the band closest to one of the metal leads. Read the bands in order from that side to find the digits, multiplier, and tolerance.

2. Why do some 5-band inductors start with a wide silver band?

A wide silver band on a 5-band inductor indicates a military-standard specification component, representing an established quality standard.

3. What is the standard unit of measurement for color-coded inductors?

Color-coded axial inductors are measured in microhenries (µH) as their base unit of calculation.

4. What does the tolerance rating on an inductor mean?

The tolerance rating represents the expected manufacturing variations in inductance, expressed as a plus or minus percentage of its nominal value.

5. How do axial inductors differ from axial resistors visually?

Though identical in physical structure, inductors are molded with a dark-green or olive body, whereas resistors typically have a beige, blue, or light gray body.

6. Can I install an axial inductor in any direction?

Yes. Inductors are non-polarized components and can be connected safely in either direction in a circuit.
