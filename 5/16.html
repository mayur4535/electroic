Diagrams & Theory

Film capacitors utilize a thin polymer plastic film (such as polyester, polypropylene, or polycarbonate) as the dielectric, layered with thin metallic electrodes.

Unlike ceramic disk capacitors, film capacitors have superior self-healing properties, exceptionally low equivalent series resistance (ESR), and high voltage stability, making them the standard choice for AC circuit networks, audio crossovers, and power supplies.

Formulas & Mathematical Logic

For 3-Digit System: Capacitance (pF) = (Digit 1 × 10 + Digit 2) × 10^Multiplier
Conversion Ratios: 1,000 pF = 1 nF | 1,000,000 pF = 1 µF = 1,000 nF

Step-by-Step Example

Example: Decodifying an "224K" Film Capacitor
Step 1: Extract the first two numbers as base values: "22".
Step 2: Identify the third digit as multiplier: "4" (representing 10^4 = 10,000).
Step 3: Multiply base value by multiplier: 22 × 10,000 = 220,000 pF.
Step 4: Convert units: 220,000 pF = 220 nF = 0.22 µF with a ±10% precision rating.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does the tolerance letter 'J' stand for in film capacitors?

The tolerance letter 'J' stands for a manufacturing deviation allowance of ±5% from the nominal capacitance rating.

2. Why are polypropylene capacitors preferred in audio crossovers?

Polypropylene caps have extremely low dielectric absorption, low dissipation factor, and minimum distortion profiles, preserving signal purity in acoustic speaker systems.

3. Are film capacitors polarized?

No, standard metalized polyester and film capacitors are non-polarized components. They can be safely wired in both AC and DC orientations.

4. How is tolerance range calculated?

The range represents the minimum and maximum boundaries of actual capacity. For example, a 100 nF capacitor with ±10% tolerance is guaranteed to measure between 90 nF and 110 nF.
