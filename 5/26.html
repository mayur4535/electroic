Glass Fuse Color Code Specifications

First / Second BandsRepresent the significant numerical values of the current rating. Decoded in Milliamperes (mA) or Amperes (A).
Multiplier BandMultiplies the significant digits to determine total current capacity. Accounts for fractional ratings with gold/silver bands.
Voltage / Speed BandTypically designates standard maximum working voltages (e.g. 125V or 250V) or speed characteristics (Fast-acting vs. Time-delay).
Single Dot MarkingCertain compact glass fuses use a single colored identifier indicating predefined current limits defined by regional manufacturers.

EIA/JEDEC Glass Fuse Marking Systems

Glass fuses protect delicate circuits against overcurrent conditions. To print indicators on clear curved glass envelopes, a durable color banding system conforming to EIA/JEDEC standards was introduced.

The sequence is read from left to right, starting with the band closest to one of the metal terminal caps. The calculated current is typically represented in milliamperes (mA) for lower limits, or directly in Amperes (A) for higher capacities.

Formulas & Calculation Logic

Current Rating (A or mA) = (Digit 1 × 10 + Digit 2) × Multiplier
Values below 1000 milliamperes are displayed in mA. Larger outputs automatically simplify to standard Amperes (A).

Step-by-Step 4-Band Example

Example Bands: Red, Red, Red, Gold
Step 1: Digit 1 (Red) = 2 | Digit 2 (Red) = 2. Combined Base = 22.
Step 2: Multiplier (Red) = x100. Base calculation: 22 × 100 = 2200 mA.
Step 3: Simplify notation: 2200 mA / 1000 = 2.2 Amps.
Step 4: Speed/Voltage marker (Gold) indicates Fast-Acting, rated up to 125V AC.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Why do some glass fuses only have a single color dot?

A single dot represents a standardized commercial code indicating the rating directly. This approach is common on subminiature fuses where standard multi-band spacing isn't visually feasible.

2. What is the difference between fast-acting and slow-blow color markings?

Fast-acting glass fuses melt immediately when a current surge is detected. Slow-blow (time-delay) fuses withstand short starter-motor surges and are marked with a distinct fourth-band configuration.

3. Can I replace a glass fuse with a ceramic version of the same color code?

If physical sizes, voltage ratings, and amperage ratings match, ceramic is typically a safe upgrade as ceramic offers a higher bursting threshold in severe high-short-circuit currents.
