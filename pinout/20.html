Logic and Power Rail Theory

The Particle Tracker, Boron, and B-Series modules utilize Nordic Semiconductor nRF52840 ARM Cortex-M4 architectures combined with cellular coprocessors. Operating at a 3.3V CMOS logic rail, these platforms can be damaged if exposed to 5V inputs. Always use active logic-level converters when interfacing with high-voltage sensors.

Tip: Use a shared common ground (GND) across all boards in your system to prevent ground loops and serial data corruption.

Formulas & Mathematical Logic

Series Current Limiting: R_series = (3.3V - V_led) / I_target (Keep under 4-8mA per pin)
I2C Pull-Up Range: R_min = (3.3V - V_low) / I_sink, preventing overcurrent drain on open-drain pins.
RF Transmission Match: Coaxial PCB traces must maintain a characteristic impedance of exactly 50 Ohms to minimize signal reflection.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Are Particle GPIO pins 5V tolerant?

No, the logic pins operate strictly at a 3.3V threshold. Exposing any GPIO, SPI, or UART pins to a 5V logic source without a level shifter can cause immediate hardware failure.

2. What is the maximum safe input voltage for the VIN pin?

The on-board power management ICs (PMIC) on the Tracker SoM and Evaluation boards can accept external power inputs ranging from 3.88V to 12.0V DC safely.

3. How do I configure the on-board cellular GPS antenna?

Ensure your coaxial RF trace connects directly to the GNSS_RF (Pin 94) or CELL_GPS_RF (Pin 87) pads with a characteristic impedance of 50 Ohms for optimal signal reception.

4. Why do the SPI and I2C lines share pins with GPIOs?

The nRF52840 chip features a flexible pin routing multiplexer, allowing software to reconfigure digital pins to support SPI, UART, I2C, or standard GPIO tasks dynamically.

5. How do I reset search filters in this tool?

Clear any text from the search box at the top of the interface to restore the full list of pinout cards.
