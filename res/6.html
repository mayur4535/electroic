Diagrams & Theory

Resistors are not manufactured in simple linear increments (e.g., 10, 20, 30 Ω). Instead, they are spaced logarithmically to ensure that the ratio between consecutive values remains constant relative to the component's tolerance rating. This system is known as the Preferred Number Series or Renard Series.

Formulas & Mathematical Logic

Logarithmic Spacing Formula: Value = 10^(n/E)
Geometric Progression step factor: R_step = 10^(1/E)
Percentage Deviation (Error): Error = 100 x (R_closest - R_desired) / R_desired

For example, the E12 series divides each decade into 12 log steps, which are rounded to standard two-digit values. This ensures there are no overlapping tolerance zones between adjacent components.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What are E-series resistor standards?

E-series are standardized sets of preferred values established by the EIA (Electronic Industries Alliance). The number following the 'E' (e.g. E6, E24, E96) indicates the number of logarithmic steps within each decade.

2. Why are resistors spaced logarithmically instead of linearly?

Logarithmic spacing ensures that the percentage difference between consecutive values remains constant relative to the component's tolerance. This prevents gaps or redundant overlaps in manufacturing.

3. Which EIA series is most commonly used?

The E24 series (5% tolerance) is the standard for general-purpose carbon film resistors. The E96 series (1% tolerance) is widely used for precision metal film resistors in sensitive analog applications.

4. How is the standard resistor value error calculated?

The percentage error is calculated by dividing the difference between the standard value and your desired value by the desired value, then multiplying by 100.

5. What happens if my selected resistor value has too high of an error?

A high resistance error can cause circuit instabilities, such as drifting amplifier gains, shifted voltage divider nodes, or incorrect timing periods in oscillator networks.
