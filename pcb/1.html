Formulas & Mathematical Logic

Step 1: Convert width, length and thickness to centimeters using selected unit multipliers.
Step 2: Convert temperature to Celsius. °C stays same, K becomes K - 273.15, °F becomes 100 / 180 × (°F - 32).
Step 3: Copper resistivity constant = 17 × 10^-7 Ω·cm.
Step 4: Copper temperature coefficient = 0.0039 per °C.
Step 5: Cross-sectional area effect = width × thickness. Larger area lowers PCB trace resistance.
Step 6: Resistance = resistivity × length / (width × thickness) × (1 + coefficient × (temperature - 25)).
Step 7: Practical meaning: long and thin PCB traces create more copper resistance, while wider and thicker traces reduce voltage drop and heat.

About This Calculator

Estimate copper resistance in PCB traces before layout problems appear.

The CalcBoy Trace Resistance Calculator helps calculate PCB copper trace resistance using trace width, trace length, copper thickness and operating temperature.

In PCB design, a copper trace is not a perfect conductor. It behaves like a small resistor, and that resistance becomes important when the board carries high current or uses very low supply voltage. A narrow trace in a 5 V regulator output, LED driver, motor controller or battery charging board can cause voltage drop, extra heating and unstable operation. This calculator gives a quick way to estimate that resistance before the PCB is manufactured.

The calculation is useful for printed circuit board design, power electronics, embedded hardware, SMPS layouts, battery-powered devices, automotive electronics and repair work. You can compare different trace widths, board copper thickness values and operating temperatures to see how the copper path changes. This is especially helpful when choosing between 1 oz copper, thicker copper, wider routing or a polygon pour for current-heavy nets.

Temperature is included because copper resistance increases when the board gets hot. A trace that looks safe at room temperature may show higher resistance near MOSFETs, power resistors, regulators or transformers. By entering a realistic operating temperature, the calculator gives a more practical estimate for real PCB conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is PCB trace resistance?

PCB trace resistance is the electrical resistance of a copper track on a printed circuit board. It depends on trace length, width, copper thickness and temperature.

Does a wider PCB trace reduce resistance?

Yes. A wider trace increases copper cross-sectional area, which lowers resistance and reduces voltage drop.

Why does copper thickness matter?

Thicker copper gives more conductor area. This reduces trace resistance and helps carry higher current with less heating.

Why is temperature included?

Copper resistance increases as temperature rises. Hot PCB areas near power parts can have higher trace resistance than room-temperature calculations.

Can this calculator be used for high-current PCB design?

Yes, it helps estimate resistance, but high-current PCB design should also consider trace temperature rise, copper pours, vias, connectors and safety margin.

What unit should I use for PCB trace width?

Mil is commonly used in PCB layout, but you can also enter cm, mm, µm or inch depending on your design data.
