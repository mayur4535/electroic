Diagrams & Theory

A wire stripline uses a round conductor inside a dielectric region with reference planes. Impedance depends strongly on conductor diameter, height and dielectric constant.

Formulas & Mathematical Logic

Formula used from source code: Z0 = 377 / (2 × π × sqrt(Er)) × log(4 × H / (π × D))
Where D = wire diameter, H = substrate height, and Er = dielectric constant.
Selected diameter and height units are converted using the original source multipliers before calculation.
Practical meaning: larger wire diameter usually reduces impedance, while larger height usually increases impedance.

Step-by-Step Example

Example: wire diameter = 10 mil, substrate height = 60 mil, dielectric constant = 4.2.
The calculator converts wire diameter and substrate height using the selected unit multipliers.
The dielectric constant is used inside the square-root term.
The ratio 4 × H / (π × D) is calculated.
The natural log of that ratio is multiplied by 377 / (2 × π × sqrt(Er)).
The final characteristic impedance is displayed in ohms.

About This Calculator

Estimate impedance for round-wire stripline transmission structures.

The CalcBoy Wire Stripline Impedance Calculator calculates characteristic impedance using wire diameter, substrate height and dielectric constant.

Wire stripline impedance is useful when a round conductor is modeled inside a dielectric region with reference planes. This type of calculation can appear in RF interconnects, embedded wire structures, transmission line experiments, fixture design and special PCB geometry checks where a flat copper trace formula is not the best fit.

The result depends strongly on the conductor diameter and dielectric height. Increasing wire diameter usually lowers impedance because the conductor becomes electrically larger. Increasing the spacing to reference planes usually raises impedance. Dielectric constant also matters because a higher-permittivity material slows the wave and reduces impedance.

This calculator is intended for quick engineering estimation. For critical RF, microwave, controlled-impedance or production designs, confirm with field solver data, PCB manufacturer stack-up information, test coupons or measured impedance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is wire stripline impedance?

It is the characteristic impedance of a round conductor placed in a dielectric region with reference plane geometry.

What inputs are required?

The calculator needs wire diameter, substrate height and dielectric constant.

Does larger wire diameter lower impedance?

Generally yes. A larger conductor diameter lowers the calculated impedance in this equation.

Why does dielectric constant matter?

Higher dielectric constant reduces wave velocity and usually lowers characteristic impedance.

Can I use this for PCB production design?

Use it as an estimate. For production controlled impedance, verify with manufacturer stack-up or field solver data.

What unit is the output?

The output impedance is displayed in ohms.
